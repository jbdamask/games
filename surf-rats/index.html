HOW TO FISH MONTAUK

You draw a beach callsign — Googin, Herb, Dangler, or Spotburn. Montauk Bill already knows every hole. Outfish him before the trip clock dies.

Storms roll in sometimes. Light rain can wake the bass up. A downpour can be hot — but lightning follows. Distant strikes flash the screen; the closer the storm, the faster the flashes and the higher the chance you get hit on the rocks. Fish or flee.

Walk the shore map and pick a spot, then hit FISH HERE. Gold B on the map is Montauk Bill. You cannot fish his hole. Talk to him — he may give you a tip — then he leaves.

ARROWS / WASD move. UP wades out. DOWN comes in. SPACE casts, works the lure, climbs a boulder, or pumps a fish.

Rocky spots have boulders. Wade out and climb. Waves shove you back. Stand too far back on the rock or too far out on a pointy peak and a big one can wash you off — lose the fish if you're still hooked up.

You carry a small box. Montauk eats plugs. Fish the rocks careless and you leave hardware in them. Leave a lure out without reeling and it will hang up.

Each lure casts a different distance: BUCKTAIL shortest, DARTER a bit farther, MINNOW farther, POPPER farthest. POPPER: shake it on top (tap SPACE). DARTER: reel, and tap UP every other turn. MINNOW: slow and steady (hold SPACE). BUCKTAIL: low and slow, lift the rod now and then so it does not hang in the boulders.

Wind piles seaweed onto the onshore side. A fouled lure catches nothing. Change type (1–4) and color ([ ]) until something eats.

When you hook up, pump the rod with SPACE. Too much heat snaps the line. Too little and a shark or seal may steal the bass. Points count only when you LAND the fish. Then you let it go.

A trip is 6 hours. Two real minutes is one game hour. Click a map marker to hike the coast (hiking costs time).

Montauk Bill's favorite charity is Kids Need More — kidsneedmore.org
